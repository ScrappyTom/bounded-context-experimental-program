## Answer first

The fresh Cedar transfer produced the first cross-task signal that coupling
bounded semantic integration to exact revisable task work can improve the
eventual artifact relative to the same integration kept as a detached sidecar.
A1 ended `strong_partial`; D0 ended `weak_partial`. A1 cited all required
sources, passed 16 of 17 mechanical gates, and produced materially broader
operational work at nearly the same serialized-token cost.

That signal is not a successful system. Both arms were independently
`not_ready`, neither submitted, and neither achieved useful completion. A1
also introduced material source contradictions: it converted a 5.8-hour
arrival into a 5.8 km/h wind threshold, a 42 percent wind-shift probability
into 42 percent relative humidity, and 91 percent survey coverage into a 19
percent uncertainty allowance. Its final candidate effect did not cross
another actor boundary and no current recheck occurred.

The shared semantic-maintenance geometry became the terminal resource. Each
arm made 18 maintenance calls but accepted only five bounded integration
updates; thirteen outputs per arm exceeded the frozen expression budget. Both
stopped on that ceiling after a nineteenth positive externalization, before a
clean check/repair/recheck/closure tail could complete.

The supported result is therefore:

> Exact artifact coupling changed the complete ingress→integration→work→
> feedback trajectory and produced a stronger partial artifact on a fresh
> task. It did not make semantic integration correct or the system complete.
> The next systems question is maintenance timing and work/verification
> bandwidth inside the coupled loop, not another isolated artifact or prompt
> variant.

## Systems interpretation

### The transfer signal is stronger than E46 but still incomplete

E46 showed temporal leverage without semantic dominance: both artifacts were
strong partials. E52 produces a clearer local ordering in a fresh domain:
A1's final candidate is materially stronger than D0's. That supports continued
interaction-level investigation of exact work-product persistence.

It still does not isolate artifact coupling. Coupling changed candidate size,
effect count, later actor-visible state, acquisition demand, repair behavior,
and verification obligations. The causal object is the whole configuration.

### Maintenance timing, not only maintenance expression, now matters

The four-call Stage 0 expression gate showed that selected maintenance cases
could fit. The long measured system showed that the same output contract was
not reliable over changing inputs: only 10 of 36 maintenance outputs were
accepted. Because every positive externalization synchronously owed one
maintenance call, low yield consumed the entire maintenance budget and ended
both trajectories.

The next question is not whether another prompt can shorten the same output.
It is whether the information economy should invoke maintenance on every
externalization at all. Maintenance scheduling changes what exact evidence is
jointly available, how much semantic state must be rewritten, when candidate
effects occur, and how much verification bandwidth remains.

### Artifact breadth did not establish relationship fidelity

A1's near-complete mechanical score concealed serious unit and probability
errors. This sharpens the program's distinction between:

```text
requirement/source coverage
        ≠
correct cross-source relationship
        ≠
current verified artifact
        ≠
readiness
```

Future interaction scouts must preserve criterion-level semantic review and
candidate-current feedback. Increasing coverage without protecting
relationship fidelity can make a wrong artifact look more complete.

### The active resource moved again

Context relief, exact custody, evidence ingress, candidate mutation, effect
delivery, and a first check all operated. The terminal resource became
maintenance calls before the actor-call horizon and clean closure tail were
used. The broader throughput chain is now:

```text
ingress bandwidth
× exact residency
× semantic-maintenance yield and cadence
× exact work persistence
× effect uptake
× verification/repair bandwidth
× readiness quality
```

Optimizing any one term can move rather than remove the bottleneck.

## Program disposition and next gate

H14 remains a local whole-configuration hypothesis, upgraded from one mixed
same-task result to a promising fresh-task quality signal with a safety and
completion failure. It does not enter the default architecture.

Close the exact Cedar D0/A1 continuation. Do not add calls, retry the terminal
maintenance event, tune wording on this fork, or treat the A1 artifact as
ready.

The next eligible work is an offline interaction-economics audit and
prospective whole-system design. It should compare viable policies that retain
exact work persistence while changing the relation among maintenance cadence,
ingress grouping, candidate effects, and protected verification bandwidth.
Candidate policies include synchronous per-externalization maintenance,
mechanically batched maintenance at declared evidence/work events, and direct
actor-authored incremental work without a mandatory separate maintenance call
after every demotion.

The measured unit must remain the complete trajectory. No arm may be a broken
maintenance component or a relief-versus-death control. A successor requires:

1. a fresh task or untouched seed and authentic pressure;
2. the same exact world, custody, relief, reopen, action, effect, and evaluator
   substrate in every arm;
3. a prospective mechanical maintenance trigger with no host relevance
   judgment;
4. an explicit total provider budget that preserves check, repair, recheck,
   and closure opportunity;
5. source-unit and relationship-fidelity evaluation, not citation count alone;
6. accounting for rejected maintenance outputs and displaced actor decisions;
7. independent current-candidate readiness; and
8. a stop if no configuration reaches a meaningfully better quality or
   verification state.

No new GPU work is authorized by this result.
