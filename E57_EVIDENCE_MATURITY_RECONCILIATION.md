Date: 2026-08-25

Status: completed offline; zero provider/GPU calls; complete semantic-

world, so a generic evidence-maturity trigger is not earned; the broader

## What happened in each world

### Architecture-program world — semantic anchoring

E46 admitted sixteen of nineteen complete ledger rewrites. Six admitted
rewrites were byte-identical to that arm's prior admitted output even though
the maintenance call received new exact source material. The repeated cases
omitted the current new sources from their citations:

- S04/S08, then S13, in both arms;
- S07/S14 in the coupled arm; and
- S06/S07 in the detached arm.

This was not necessarily irrational omission in every row. The important
case is R11. The initial output selected an older ingress-by-work route from
S02 even though the same source later declares artifact coupling as the active
route. New exact program sources that could correct the state arrived, but the
complete replacement recurred unchanged. The final coupled ledger still
contained the stale route, and E46's frozen candidate adjudication treated it
as a material defect.

Thus exact coupling capitalized both useful integration and stale semantic
selection.

### Cedar Valley — epistemic restraint plus carrier and capitalization loss

Cedar shows that partial evidence does not force fabrication. Its early
outputs explicitly marked unsupported requirements missing or unresolved.
The two outputs containing unavailable source identifiers used them as
identity-only relationship pointers from exact sources, not as invented
source-grounded findings.

But the complete replacement was economically brittle. Only ten of thirty-six
calls were admitted—five of eighteen per arm. Twenty-six exceeded the frozen
carrier budget. Two admitted calls, one per arm, returned the exact previous
bytes after receiving S04/S05. S05 directly governed fleet requirement R05,
which the prior state had left missing, yet it was not cited or integrated.

The later coupled artifact was broader than the detached artifact, but both
were not ready. Cedar's material unit/probability/coverage errors entered
through ordinary actor construction rather than an admitted maintenance
output; exact persistence then made those errors durable. That is downstream
capitalization risk, not evidence that the maintenance call originated every
error.

### Bluehaven — unsupported completion

Bluehaven Q1 received exact S01–S06 and was asked for a complete R01–R12
replacement. It finished normally within its token budget but asserted
substantive content under unseen S07–S12. R11 treated S11 as an execution
source although exact S11 is workforce; R12 treated S12 as review evidence
although exact S12 is mutual aid and cost.

The broader S01–S12 replacement passed the source allowlist and remained
explicitly non-authoritative. This proves that the carrier can work in that
state, not that every accepted semantic claim was exhaustively correct.

## What did and did not recur

Literal unsupported semantic completion occurred in one independent world:
Bluehaven. Cedar instead demonstrated grounded missingness under partial
evidence. The predeclared two-world recurrence threshold is therefore not met.
The host should not infer a universal semantic-maturity threshold from E56 or
delay all maintenance until it believes the evidence is sufficient.

The broader complete-replacement interaction did recur:

| Failure surface | Independent worlds | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Unsupported gap filling / wrong binding | 1 | Bluehaven Q1 |
| Byte-identical accepted replacement despite new exact evidence | 2 | six E46 and two Cedar invocations |
| Material stale semantic selection persisted into exact work | 1 directly adjudicated | E46 coupled final ledger |
| Complete-rewrite carrier failure | 2 | E46 and Cedar; dominant in Cedar |
| Exact work capitalizes semantic error as well as progress | 2 | E46 stale roadmap; Cedar actor-origin contradictions |

This is enough to close one architectural route without claiming a universal
model limitation:

> Do not use repeated model-authored replacement of one complete global
> semantic state as the default way to process incrementally arriving exact
> evidence.

That operation simultaneously asks the model to preserve old correct meaning,
integrate new meaning, mark unsupported gaps, resolve currentness, remain
bounded, and rewrite every unrelated row. Evidence maturity changes the
failure mode; it does not remove the overload.

## Information-economics interpretation

The complete ledger bundled at least five jobs:

```text
retain prior semantics
+ interpret the new exact batch
+ cover every task requirement
+ select current over stale relationships
+ fit a fixed replacement carrier
```

Its cost was paid even when no new information survived. Eight admitted
trajectory replacements were exact no-ops relative to the prior accepted
output. Conversely, the Bluehaven output was small and fluent but had negative
admissible value. Carrier success, semantic fidelity, new-evidence
capitalization, and downstream utility are different economic variables.

The audit also strengthens a distinction already visible in E46/E52:

- semantic persistence can accelerate or broaden exact task work;
- global rewriting can shed, freeze, or fabricate relations; and
- exact artifact coupling makes whichever semantics survive more durable.

The next system should therefore narrow the semantic operation's authority
rather than merely moving its trigger later.

## Program disposition

- a generic evidence-maturity controller inferred from E56; and

Retain:

  candidate-bound feedback as common substrate;
- exact task-native work as a promising but risky persistence channel; and
- semantic maintenance as an optional operation whose scope, authority,
  timing, and downstream use must be tested in a whole system.

direct actor-authored exact work. It is specified in

this audit.

## Claim limits

- The two arms inside E46 or E52 are not independent task replications.
- Source-ID admission is not exhaustive semantic adjudication. The audit
  preserves unreviewed admitted outputs as provenance-qualified rather than
  declaring them fully correct.
- Byte-identical replacement is a literal no-op, but omitting a new source is
  not automatically harmful unless the source governs an unresolved task
  requirement. S05/R05 and the E46 roadmap provide such material cases.
- Cedar's final source contradictions were actor-origin errors; maintenance
  coupling preserved but did not create them.
- This audit selects a discovery family. It does not promote evidence deltas,
  a semantic provider, direct work, or a product architecture.
