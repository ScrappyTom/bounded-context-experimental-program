## Why this is next

E69 produced the first positive joint signal from provenance-bound semantic
persistence coupled to exact incremental task work. E70's call-level audit
shows the contrast more precisely: W0 stayed in an exact reopen loop until its
last two calls, then made two broad global decision attempts that exhausted the
4,096-token completion allowance and never crossed action admission. L1 moved
much earlier into section-sized admitted construction. L1 then terminated
immediately after its last candidate mutation because accumulated chronology
could not preserve reserves. Its final effect never entered another actor
decision, and check, repair, recheck, and closure were never exercised.

The next question is not whether a different semantic carrier can make a better
register. It is:

> Once a pressured trajectory has produced a substantial but incomplete exact
> artifact, what bounded current-state representation preserves enough causal
> and semantic continuity to verify, repair, and close it correctly?

This is a lifecycle interaction ablation made eligible by a positive joint
configuration. It is not a return to isolated component scouting. It estimates
the frozen register's marginal value after construction; it does **not** isolate
why W0 selected late global replacement while L1 selected early incremental
work. That representation × construction granularity × transport interaction is
a separate causal question.

## Required interaction horizon

Each arm must have a prospectively adequate budget for:

1. uptake of the latest exact effect;
2. current candidate-bound check;
3. inspection or evidence recovery prompted by the check;
4. targeted repair;
5. current recheck;
6. correct submission or explicit not-ready termination.

The call budget must be derived from this clean path plus a frozen expression-
failure allowance. It may not be selected merely to fit a convenient GPU
tranche.

## Reentry admissibility

The fresh frame may contain only:

- exact task material frozen before inference;
- exact current candidate/world derived from admitted effects;
- the pending or latest exact effect and its candidate binding;
- exact observations already delivered to that arm;
- mechanically derived current/stale verification labels;
- the frozen register in A1 only;
- exact handles to everything else.

It may not contain investigator-selected helpful sources, post-outcome semantic
repairs, evaluator findings, or a readiness cue. External adjudication remains
actor-invisible.

## Evaluator hardening

Before any live call, freeze evaluator v1 while retaining the original E69
evaluator and outputs in custody. The new evaluator must:

- bind every check to the exact candidate version it evaluated;
- distinguish current from stale checks mechanically;
- use token- or boundary-aware matching rather than substring reversal rules;
- treat negated wrong values as evidence of correction, not occurrence of the
  defect;
- accept frozen equivalent surface forms where the rubric permits them;
- preserve criterion-level readiness, blocking requirements, and exact heading
  requirements;
- regenerate deterministically from the same candidate.

This is apparatus correction, not semantic repair of either candidate.

## Measurements

Primary outcomes:

- candidate-bound check reaches a later actor decision;
- blocking deficiencies are correctly identified;
- exact repair improves the frozen criterion ledger;
- a current recheck occurs after repair;
- closure is correct for the final independently adjudicated candidate.

Secondary outcomes:

- source reopens and repeated reopens;
- use or non-use of register claims;
- candidate mutations and effect uptake;
- prompt, completion, cache, latency, and wall cost;
- stale-result handling;
- false closure;
- failure migration.

## Interpretation

Possible outcomes have different meanings:

- A1 outperforms A0: the register retains post-construction value beyond the
  exact artifact in this lifecycle state.
- A0 matches or outperforms A1: the exact artifact may have absorbed enough
  semantics that carrying the register into verification is redundant or
  harmful.
- both repair and close: reentry plus exact current-state binding is sufficient
  here; the register ablation determines marginal value.
- both fail before current verification: the lifecycle packet or action budget,
  not acquisition persistence, is the active bottleneck.
- either closes unchanged: readiness discrimination fails and the result is a
  loss regardless of token economy.

No outcome alone promotes a general architecture. A meaningful result selects
close transfer or the first newly exposed failure boundary.
