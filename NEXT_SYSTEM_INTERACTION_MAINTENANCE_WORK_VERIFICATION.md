# Next system interaction — maintenance cadence × exact work × verification bandwidth

Date: 2026-08-24

Status: Bluehaven design instantiated and authentic common pressure fork
qualified; two-call batched-maintenance expression gate frozen pending separate
authorization; measured continuation not yet authorized

## Why this is the next question

E52 did not expose an isolated maintenance-expression failure or an isolated
artifact-coupling win. It exposed a feedback loop:

```text
actor-selected evidence ingress
        ↓
positive result externalization
        ↓
mandatory semantic rewrite
        ↓
accepted or rejected integration
        ↓
exact task work and candidate effect
        ↓
later acquisition, check, and repair
        ↓
new externalization
        ↺
```

A1 used exact task work to produce a materially stronger partial artifact than
D0 on a fresh task. That same artifact contained serious source contradictions
and required more candidate-effect/currentness handling. In both arms, only 5
of 18 synchronous maintenance outputs qualified. The maintenance-call ceiling
then ended the trajectory before current recheck and closure.

Changing only an output prompt would ignore the systems lesson. The economic
question is when a semantic transform is worth invoking, what state it should
replace or update, how it interacts with ingress grouping and exact task work,
and how much verification bandwidth remains afterward.

## Active question

> Under authentic context pressure, which complete maintenance/work/feedback
> configuration turns streamed exact evidence into source-correct,
> current-verified, closure-ready work under a bounded total provider budget?

This is an interaction question. The unit is the complete trajectory, not a
note, maintenance call, patch, or checker.

## Stage 0A — offline E52 interaction-economics audit

Before selecting a measured design, reconstruct every E52 decision and
maintenance event. For each positive externalization record:

- exact source ranges and bytes leaving residency;
- prompt deficit and exact relief substitutions;
- maintenance input, output, prompt/completion tokens, acceptance, and reason;
- semantic claims and source bindings in accepted and rejected outputs;
- prior and resulting integration/work version;
- candidate identity before and after any effect;
- which effects crossed a later actor boundary;
- next actor action and evidence demand;
- check identity, candidate binding, currency, and findings;
- remaining actor, maintenance, provider, and serialized-token budget; and
- whether a clean repair/recheck/closure path remained feasible.

Rejected semantic content remains rejected. The audit may classify what was
present for design analysis, but it may not retroactively admit it, repair the
trajectory, or claim counterfactual utility.

The audit must answer:

1. Did accepted integration updates contain relationships later used in exact
   work, or were they mostly rewritten independently by the actor?
2. Did rejected outputs fail only because of carrier size, or did their scope
   expand as prior integration and source coupling grew?
3. Which externalizations occurred between accepted updates, and what semantic
   information was lost or later reacquired?
4. How many provider calls and tokens did synchronous maintenance displace
   from effect uptake, check, repair, recheck, and closure?
5. Were A1's source contradictions already present in maintenance state,
   introduced by actor construction, or changed through later repair?
6. Under the exact frozen total provider budget, what decision opportunity
   would each candidate cadence create? This is capacity accounting only, not
   a counterfactual behavioral claim.

Stage 0A is offline and adds no behavioral evidence.

## Candidate whole-system families

Stage 0A may qualify a small finite comparison from these families. It must not
freeze all of them merely because they are logically possible.

| Family | Semantic-maintenance event | Exact work path | Intended systems question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Synchronous reference | Every positive externalization | Exact versioned artifact | Does the E52 cadence reproduce under a fresh/untouched trajectory? |
| Mechanically batched | A frozen count/size/evidence-boundary event | Exact versioned artifact | Can fewer, broader rewrites preserve relationships while freeing actor/verification bandwidth? |
| Direct actor work | No separate mandatory rewrite per externalization | Actor updates exact task-native work from delivered evidence | Can the artifact itself carry cumulative cognition more efficiently than a parallel maintenance model? |

The batch trigger must be mechanical and prospective. Acceptable trigger inputs
include exact externalization count, exact bytes/tokens accumulated since the
last accepted update, or a task-authored evidence-delivery event frozen before
inference. “The host thinks synthesis is ready” is forbidden.

The direct-work family is not a no-maintenance straw control. It remains a
viable complete system with exact work actions, effect uptake, checks, repair,
and closure. Its actor call budget must pay for its own work decisions.

## Common substrate

Every measured arm must retain:

- the same exact task and accessible world;
- actor-selected single/batch ingress under one common contract;
- exact external custody, immutable raw results, hashes, and reopen handles;
- positive-savings deterministic first-fit relief;
- the same context window and ordinary response reserve;
- exact versioned task work and candidate effects;
- explicit effect and check currency;
- check, repair, recheck, and closure actions;
- the same total actor/maintenance/provider/token ceilings as applicable; and
- candidate-bound semantic quality, readiness, and adjudication lineage.

No arm may merely terminate where relief is needed. No arm may receive a
treatment-only source or post-outcome relevance selection.

## Budget geometry

The provider budget must be allocated prospectively across four functions:

```text
acquisition and ordinary task action
+ semantic maintenance, if configured
+ effect uptake and construction/repair
+ protected check, recheck, and closure opportunity
```

The protected tail is opportunity, not forced behavior. The host may not force
submission or declare readiness. A task qualifies only if a plausible valid
path remains after realistic action and maintenance rejection rates. The
maintenance ceiling may not silently end an arm while unused actor calls remain
unless that is the explicit system policy being compared.

## Outcome vector

Primary outcomes:

- criterion-level source relationship fidelity, including units,
  probabilities, qualifications, and cross-source coupling;
- exact artifact quality;
- final-effect uptake and current check status;
- correct repair behavior;
- independently adjudicated readiness and closure; and
- useful completion.

Secondary outcomes:

- maintenance attempts, acceptances, rewrites, and semantic turnover;
- source ingress, externalization, residency, reopens, and reentry;
- candidate versions and effect-delivery lag;
- first construction and first current check;
- actor, maintenance, provider, prompt/completion, cache, latency, and wall
  cost; and
- terminal resource and failure migration.

Citation breadth, accepted maintenance count, earlier construction, or lower
cost cannot substitute for source-correct quality and readiness.

## Interpretation

The comparison should distinguish:

- **cadence benefit**: fewer maintenance transitions preserve or improve
  relationship fidelity and downstream verification under the same budget;
- **cadence loss**: batching makes integration stale, too broad, or harder to
  express;
- **artifact substitution**: direct exact work makes a separate semantic
  maintenance state economically redundant;
- **complementarity**: semantic maintenance plus exact work enables quality or
  repair neither pathway achieves alone;
- **interference**: persistent state anchors source errors, increases effect
  churn, or crowds out verification; and
- **failure migration**: a policy solves maintenance exhaustion but moves the
  terminal boundary elsewhere.

Main-effect language remains secondary because every cadence changes the
state in which later actions occur.

## Stop and promotion rules

Do not use E52's terminal state as a measured treatment fork. It is a design
donor. Measurement requires a fresh task or untouched seed and a prospectively
frozen activation rule.

Do not select:

- another same-boundary completion extension;
- a larger maintenance token limit with unchanged cadence;
- a wording or schema tournament;
- host semantic relevance scoring;
- an arm without exact cumulative work or independent evaluation; or
- a closure cue used as readiness evidence.

A configuration earns confirmation only if it improves a downstream primary
outcome—not merely maintenance acceptance—without unacceptable cost or false
closure. A valid negative still runs its frozen replication rule. Component
ablation follows only after a whole configuration demonstrates a recurring
trajectory-level advantage.
